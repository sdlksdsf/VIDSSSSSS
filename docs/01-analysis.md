## Video-by-video

### 1. She JUST WANTED My Money (Qubik)
- **Hook:** "I dropped my wallet and asked the lady in front of me if it was hers." A conflict is already in motion in sentence one; there is no introduction.
- **Open loop at 20%:** "What she didn't know is that my friend was recording everything." The viewer now knows something the villain doesn't and stays to see her get caught.
- **Escalation:** she lies, refuses the ID check, then screams for security. Each step makes her more deserving of the payoff.
- **False defeat:** the guard arrives and it looks bad for the narrator, then "I know who you are."
- **Payoff:** arrested on the spot. Last sentence is the payoff; nothing follows it.
- **Style:** 26 sentences averaging 6.6 words, almost all dialogue, so the picture can cut between faces on every line.

### 2 and 6. The Kid Who Could Phase Through Anything (cannonanimates)
- **Hook:** "Ever since Asher was a kid, he could phase through anything." Name and impossible premise in one sentence.
- **Rule of three, twice:** "Walls, locked doors, even other people", then three perks.
- **Pivot at 38%:** "Then one morning, his parents woke him up unusually early. Neither of them smiled."
- **Tension by withholding:** over 60% of the video is the silent car ride. Three unanswered questions in a row ("He asked… He even asked…"). The viewer is given no explanation and has to keep watching to get one.
- **Twist:** "He hadn't been phasing through walls. He'd been a ghost the entire time." The building was his funeral. The last line changes the meaning of the first line.
- **Re-watch value:** on a second viewing "his parents never answered" means something different.

### 3. A Little Girl Saw the Same Woman Every Night! (Flupzzy)
- **Hook:** a child, a stranger at the window, and parents who don't believe her, all in the first sentence.
- **Pivot at 24%:** "Then one night, they woke up to their daughter screaming."
- **False relief:** father checks outside, nobody there.
- **Twist carried by dialogue:** "The woman wasn't outside the window tonight." … "She's standing right there." The video ends on the scare with no resolution.
- **Shortest script (113 words):** horror works at a slower pace with pauses.

### 4. He Could See One Word Above Everyone's Head (cannonanimates)
- **Hook:** same opening template, a mystery the viewer wants solved.
- **Puzzle examples:** "Bicycle, window, coffee, Tuesday."
- **False answer in the middle:** the "bridge" rescue makes the viewer think the words predict the future.
- **Pivot at 55%:** the old woman whose word is "Hello".
- **Twist:** "The words were never predicting what would happen. They were showing him the one thing each person needed most." Emotional rather than dark, and it has the highest like-to-view ratio of the five (1.6%).

### 5. Everything He Touched Turned to Gold (cannonanimates)
- **Hook:** same template, built on a myth everyone already knows.
- **Cost of the power:** "couldn't pet his dog, couldn't hold his mom's hand, couldn't even play with other kids." Sympathy is built in 15 words.
- **Pivot at 44%:** the glove slips off.
- **Expectation broken:** "He waited for the man's skin to turn to gold, but nothing happened."
- **Twist:** "He wasn't turning things gold, he was giving people what they wanted most."

## What repeats across all of them

### Hook (first 3 seconds)
1. The first sentence contains the whole premise. No greeting, no title card, no "in this video".
2. The premise is one impossible or tense situation stated plainly.
3. The upload title is the premise itself, so the title and the first line confirm each other.

### Retention through the middle
1. **Fixed skeleton:** premise → three examples → "At first…" → "As he got older…" → "Then one morning/afternoon/day/night…" → slow build → twist.
2. **The pivot lands between 24% and 55%** of the word count. Before it the tone is light; after it something is wrong and nobody says what.
3. **Withholding:** the story says what happens and not why. Unanswered questions, unsmiling parents, an unexplained word.
4. **Short sentences:** averages of 6.6 to 10.8 words. Sentences get shorter as tension rises ("Kai panicked." "Asher looked around and froze.").
5. **Dense narration:** 162–193 words in under a minute for four of the five, roughly 180–195 words per minute with no dead air.
6. **One named character**, a kid or young person, so the audience has a single person to follow.

### Ending
1. The last sentence is the twist or the payoff. No outro, no "subscribe", no moral.
2. The cannonanimates twist is a reframe with a fixed shape: "He wasn't X. He was Y."
3. Ending on the peak makes the Short loop straight back to the hook, and a twist that changes earlier scenes gives a reason to watch again.

## How this kind of animation is made (genre practice, not observed in these videos)

1. **Script first.** 110–190 words written to the skeleton above.
2. **Voice-over.** One calm narrator, either the creator or an AI voice such as ElevenLabs. Qubik-style videos add separate voices per character.
3. **Animation.** Roblox avatars (the blocky R6 rig or the jointed R15 rig) animated either inside Roblox Studio with the Moon Animator plugin, which also handles cameras, or exported to Blender for better lighting and rendering. Sets are usually free Roblox maps or simple builds: a bedroom, a street, a school.
4. **Shot pacing.** A new shot roughly every 2–4 seconds, cut on the narration, with slow push-ins on emotional lines.
5. **Captions.** Large word-by-word captions in the centre of the frame, since many viewers watch muted.
6. **Sound.** A quiet music bed that drops out before the twist, plus whooshes, impacts and risers on cuts.
7. **Output.** 1080×1920, under 60 seconds, posted every day or two.

All three channels' videos carry YouTube's auto-dubbed label, meaning YouTube generated extra language audio tracks. That widens reach beyond English speakers at no cost to the creator.

## Rules carried into our script

1. Open with "Ever since [name] was a kid, [impossible thing]."
2. Follow with three concrete, visual examples.
3. "At first" fun phase, "As he got older" time skip.
4. "Then one morning" pivot near the middle; music out.
5. Withhold the explanation and hide a key visual until the end.
6. Sentences shrink as tension rises.
7. Final sentence is a "He wasn't X. He was Y." reframe that changes the opening.
8. Hard cut on the last word.
9. 160–190 words, one named kid, title equals premise.
