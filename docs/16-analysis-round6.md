## The winning formula (identical in all six, including the top two at 9M+/9.6M)

**1. Hook = one bizarre rule, stated in sentence 1, shown on screen in the first second.**
- "In this town, every secret you say out loud turns into a gray mouse."
- "In this town, nobody is afraid of anything because if you fall, you respawn."
- "In this town, everything is gray and whatever you paint becomes yours."
- "In this world, everyone has a narrator."
- Conflict-driven openers work too: "I was struggling to breathe, so I reached for my oxygen mask."
- The rule IS the hook — no intro, no "hey guys", no "stop scrolling". A scroller sees the
  impossible rule and wants the world explained.

**2. Instant consequence beat (5–12 s).** One concrete, funny, visual example of the rule
in action: 300 mice chasing a girl through the cafeteria; kids jumping off roofs; one tiny
drop of paint a year; a doctor doing crosswords because the hospital closed.

**3. "Me" enters by line 3–5.** The narrator is a kid in the world with a personality
(the best secret keeper, the only careful kid, the paint thief, the kid who never forgets).

**4. A question is planted and answered only at the end.** Will he break his promise to
keep Milo's secrets? Will the robot own the town? Will the narrator ever speak? The viewer
stays for the answer.

**5. Escalation with a twist.** The protagonist tries the obvious fix and fails, each
failure bigger and more comic (mouse frozen in 10 steps, 20, 30; "buddy", "dude", "best
friend" — the wrong names; the robot painting every house black).

**6. Emotional core, small and human.** The rule-break is for a person: a lost best
friend, a grandpa who lost his checkpoint, a drowning girl, a crush. The final secret /
truth is the most honest line in the script ("I've been scared for 3 years that I'm not
his best friend", "Grandpa hadn't felt safe since Grandma left").

**7. Joke or reversal every ~8 seconds.** "He's trying to break the record", "the doctors
just do crosswords", "Milo's cat fainted", "I've scrubbed 312 houses. Only 4,000 to go",
"I'm kidding, idiot", a kid farting into the air filter.

**8. Hard cut ending on a punchline.** No outro, no CTA, no "thanks for watching", no
"watch till the end", no subscribe ask. The last line is the payoff that rewards watching
to the end.

**9. Length.** ~135–300 words. The two 9M+ hits are ~210–265 words (≈55–65 s). The
529K outlier is the longest (300 words) and the slowest to get to "me".

**10. Voice.** One narrator telling from "I" — calm, human, unhurried, slightly deadpan.
Character lines are short and snappy. Dialogue jokes land harder than narration jokes.

## Picture / animation style (inferred from thumbnails + the format; frames themselves were
not watched — no video download available)

- Roblox-style blocky characters with smooth, exaggerated movements and clear facial
  expressions; bright saturated colours; wide establishing shots of the town, tighter
  shots on faces for dialogue.
- Visual gags match the words exactly (gray mice popping in smoke puffs, gold sparkles,
  gray world being painted black, a blurred face).
- Quick camera work between beats; sound effects on every gag (poof, crash, sparkle).
- Consistent character design: hoodie kid protagonist, supporting kids, authority figure
  (fire chief, mayor, teacher) as the "straight man" for the last line.

## What to change for Short #6 (vs. our Shorts 1–3)

- Copy the "rule-as-hook" opening — Shorts 1–3 used incident-first openings; the samples
  prove the town-rule hook is the stronger 5-second strategy for this format.
- Keep a hard-cut punchline ending (Shorts 2/3 already do).
- Several voices, short lines (rule 5 stands).
- New background music + SFX layer, smoother camera transitions, improved lighting/effects
  (user requirement for this Short and all future Shorts).
- Human-like narration: natural pace, no flat AI delivery; audition fresh voices.
- Keep the recurring channel character: the narrator should be **Red**, the kid in the red
  hoodie (from Shorts 2/3), so the channel builds a recognizable hero.

## Short #6 concept (original, not any sample's rule)

**"In this town, when you forget a person's name, their face goes blurry — and they can't
hear you until you say it again."**

Original, instantly visual, has built-in comedy (people wandering around talking to smudges)
and a built-in emotional trap (the one person whose name you never say out loud). Stake:
the best friend is trapped under rubble and can only hear his real name.
