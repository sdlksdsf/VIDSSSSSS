## How they are built
- One narrator, no characters, no dialogue. 30-45 seconds, 110-125 words.
- Sentence one is the whole promise: "There is only one country that...", "places you're banned from", "who would win?".
- Short flat sentences, one fact each, a number in most of them (1967, 34, 171, 150 ships).
- Picture (inferred): satellite or flat map, zoom to a place, country lit up in a colour, arrows, labels, flags, a new image every 2-3 seconds, big captions.
- No intro, no outro, no call to action.

## What decides the views
- The biggest one (33M) hides the answer until the final word, so leaving early means not knowing. The weakest (2.5M) is five unrelated facts: nothing is owed to the viewer, so they can leave after any item.
- "You" in the hook (banned from) and a contest with a winner (what-if) sit in between.
- Rule for ours: one question in sentence one, answered by the last sentence, with a second surprise after the answer.

## Accuracy
Some sample claims are loose or wrong (the US and Australian coasts do not "almost perfectly match"; Snake Island is about 33 km off the coast). Ours uses only facts that hold up.
