# Round 3 — our own numbers, four new samples, and what changes

Method and limits: transcripts of the four new Shorts were read in full, plus YouTube page data where the page returned it. I still cannot play the videos, so notes on picture are inferred from the scripts. The benchmark ranges below are the ones creators commonly quote, not official YouTube figures.

## 1. What our first Short's numbers say

"The Kid Who Could Stop Time" (59 s): 803 engaged views, **61.5% stayed to watch / 38.5% swiped away**, **average view duration 0:36** (about 61% of the video).

- Almost 4 in 10 people left at the start. Shorts that get pushed widely are usually quoted at 70–85%+ "stayed to watch". The opening is the first problem.
- People who stayed left on average a little past half way. Viral Shorts are usually quoted near or above 100% average viewed (people rewatch). The middle is the second problem.
- Why, compared with the samples:
  1. Our first line was a description ("Ever since Eli was a kid, he could stop time"). Nobody was in trouble, so there was no reason to wait for anything.
  2. Our middle was a list of montage examples with no opponent and no rising stakes.
  3. One narrator voice for 59 seconds. The big samples change voice every few seconds.
  4. The ending was sad and quiet. Nothing to laugh at, quote or rewatch.

### The skeleton (samples 1–3)

1. **Sentence one puts "me" in trouble.** "I was driving slowly when a woman jumped in front of my car and threw herself on the ground, even though my car never touched her." "I was taking a test when the annoying kid… tied a bunch of balloons to the teacher's chair." Ordinary moment + outrageous act by someone else, in a single breath.
2. **A false accusation inside the first 5 seconds.** "He hit me! Call the cops!" / "Purple. I told him not to." The viewer now has a question that can only be answered at the end: does the liar get away with it?
3. **A villain who lies "confidently".** The word is used again and again. The villain never doubts, which makes the viewer want to see them lose.
4. **A comeback every 6–8 seconds.** "You're more likely to die from that guy bumping into you." "He's running away by standing still?" Each one is a small reward, so there is never 8 seconds without a payoff.
5. **The crowd reacts.** "Everyone around us started laughing. Her face went red." Background characters tell the viewer how to feel.
6. **It gets worse before it gets better.** The cops arrive / "Purple, you're in big trouble." The hero is about to lose.
7. **"That's when I noticed…"** A small detail that was on screen earlier (the dent, the paper on the desk).
8. **The villain convicts themselves with their own mouth.** "That dent was already there before I threw myself on the ground." "I didn't tie all those balloons to your chair so you could watch me cheat."
9. **An authority figure delivers the last joke.** "You have the right to remain silent. You really should have used your blinker." Hard cut. No outro, no call to action.

### Writing style

- First person, past tense, very plain words. "I said", "He got mad", "She said confidently".
- Short narration, then a line of dialogue in another voice. The voice changes roughly every 3–5 seconds.
- Almost no description. Only actions and speech.
- A recurring hero with a simple name the villain can say ("Purple"), which builds a channel character.

### Sample 4 (Lying Makes You Shorter)

World-rule opening like the Gelo Stories samples, but told with jokes in every line ("My grandpa sold used cars for 40 years. We keep him in a cup."), a hero who can't let things go, a mystery ("nobody asks why"), and the same ending mechanic: the villain is undone by his own words ("I can explain." "2 inches.").

## 3. Rules I am now applying without being asked

1. First sentence: "me" + trouble + someone else's outrageous act. On screen from frame one.
2. A question planted before second 5 that only the last 5 seconds answer.
3. A confident liar as villain; crowd in the background reacting, moving, laughing.
4. A joke, comeback or reversal at least every 8 seconds.
5. Several voices; narration lines kept short.
6. "That's when I noticed" clue that was visible earlier.
7. The villain exposes themselves; an authority figure gets the last line; hard cut.
8. No "watch till the end", no "like and subscribe", no "thanks for watching", no "stop scrolling".
9. Shorter running time (about 45–50 s) so average percentage viewed goes up.
10. Bright saturated day scene, expressive faces, word-by-word captions, colour-coded per speaker.
