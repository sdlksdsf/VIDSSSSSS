## 2. Per-video breakdown

### #4 Nobody Says Their Name (12M) — the strongest sample
- **Hook (sentence 1):** "In this town, saying your name is deadly." Seven words: place, rule, stakes.
- **Gag run (next 5 sentences):** blank mailboxes, roll call with drawings, name tags "Hello, I'm ___", every dog is called Dog, cake says "Congrats, you". Each line is one picture and one joke.
- **Threat:** "Something out there collects names. If it hears yours, it comes."
- **Outsider breaks the rule:** "We moved in on a Saturday… Hello, I'm Gaylord." Three reactions in a row: mom covers baby's ears, man dives into a bush, even Dog runs away.
- **Exile:** sat alone at school, drawing crossed out.
- **Creeping escalation by day:** every morning a note; Wednesday on the second-floor window; Thursday Martha says her name and is taken.
- **Authority ultimatum:** the mayor, in his own voice.
- **Confrontation at night:** the creature appears, "It bent down and raised a finger. I shut my eyes."
- **Reframe:** "It wasn't after me." It had no name, so it borrowed everyone else's. "Names aren't taken, they're given."
- **Payoff run:** every opening gag is reversed (tags peeled, doors open, names on mailboxes).
- **Punchline + CTA in character:** the mayor's name was Pom Pom. "Like and subscribe or from today your name is Pom Pom."

### #2 You Turn to Gold When You Die
Same skeleton, sad flavour: rule → gag run that is dark instead of funny (balloons, cake and a scale at funerals) → grandpa's warning "don't celebrate" → he dies, no gold, only mud → exile (door painted black) → nightly escalation (hooded man: last streetlight, the corner, across the street) → mayor's ultimatum → midnight → "Your grandpa wasn't human." → callback "I told you not to celebrate." "I didn't. I cried." → greedy uncle hugs an empty scale → CTA in character.

### #6 and #7 Tyler — "true bizarre fact"
- Sentence 1 states the freak fact **and** opens a question: "A man had a toothache for 6 days before anyone worked out what was actually causing it."
- Numbers everywhere (6 days, 4-inch, an inch and a half, 27 hours, four arms and four legs).
- One straight line of events, no subplot, 35–40 seconds, ends on the answer with no call to action — the video stops at peak interest, which pushes replays.

### #5 Little Girl / Woman at the window
Covered in round 1: disbelief → one night it changes → the twist is a single change of location ("She's standing right there"), cut on the last word.

### #1 Ding Dong Ditch
No narration at all. A universally understood prank, told with body language, timing and sound effects; the only "line" is a repeated "Huh?". It proves the animation and the sound alone must be able to carry a joke.

## 3. The shared strategy

**Writing**
1. **Rule first.** Sentence 1 is a rule of the world plus stakes, not a character introduction.
2. **Gag run.** Immediately 4–6 one-line examples of daily life under that rule. One sentence = one shot = one joke. This is the retention engine for seconds 3–15.
3. **A legend with a threat** ("They say…", "Something out there…").
4. **An outsider breaks the rule by accident** ("We moved in on Sunday and nobody told us the rule").
5. **Rule of three** for reactions, always ending on the silliest one (the dog).
6. **Exile + nickname** makes the hero sympathetic in two sentences.
7. **"Something felt off"** — clues planted that only make sense after the twist.
8. **Days of the week** move time fast (Wednesday… Thursday…), and a named side character gets taken.
9. **The mayor**: a quoted ultimatum in a second voice.
10. **Midnight, alone**: lights go out one by one, the thing arrives, the hero shuts their eyes.
11. **Reframe**: "It wasn't X. It was Y." The monster is lonely, itchy or kind; the town was wrong.
12. **Payoff run**: every opening gag is reversed in one line each.
13. **Mayor punchline** exposing why he loved the rule, then an optional call to action spoken as a joke from the story.
14. **Style**: first person, past tense, average 6–7 words per sentence, concrete nouns, almost no adjectives.

**Sound** (from transcript cues)
- Narrator plus 2–3 character voices (mayor, victim, creature).
- Music under most of it; music marks are logged throughout the Tyler and ItzAceNoob tracks.
- Single reaction sounds do the work of a sentence ("Oh no.", "Huh?", a phone ringing, locks falling "one by one").

**Picture** (inferred)
- A new shot roughly every 1.5–2.5 seconds, each showing exactly what the sentence names.
- **Background life in every shot**: townspeople doing the rule-gag behind the hero (people on stilts, a man diving into a bush, the dog). The background is where the extra jokes live.
- Roblox-style characters with expressive decal faces; simple props that read instantly (scale, marker, mailbox).

## 4. Colour theme (inferred — please confirm or send screenshots)

| Part of the story | Palette | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Day / gag run | Saturated grass green, clear sky blue, white clouds, warm yellow and orange buildings, one strong accent colour on the hero (red or yellow) | Bright, toy-like, instantly "Roblox" |
| Exile / "something felt off" | Same colours, slightly cooler and longer shadows | Mood shift without losing colour |
| Night confrontation | Deep blue and purple, with warm yellow from windows and streetlights; the creature in pure black or ink | Highest contrast for the reveal |
| Resolution | Golden-hour warm light, back to full saturation | Relief |

The constant across the samples is **complementary pairs**: green/blue world with warm yellow-orange lights, and a dark creature against a glowing background.

## 5. What changes in our next short

- Switch from the "kid with a power" template to the **world-rule fable** template (the 12M one).
- First person, multi-voice, faster delivery.
- Gag run in seconds 2–9, background life in every shot.
- A story set at night in a town full of lights, so the picture can be saturated and glowing from the first frame.
