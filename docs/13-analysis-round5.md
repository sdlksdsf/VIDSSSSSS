## What type of video
- One calm male narrator explains ONE process or ONE true story, 20-35 seconds, 60-95 words.
- No intro, no outro, no call to action, no jokes. The video ends on the last step of the process.
- Titles are plain: "How X Works" + one emoji.

## How the script is built
- First words put the viewer inside it: "When you get stitches...", "If you get a splinter...". The story one opens mid-action: "The customers suddenly attacked her".
- Strict order of events: this happens, so this happens, then this. Linked with "as", "then", "afterwards", "and over a few weeks".
- Every sentence is something you can SEE. No opinions, no numbers lists.
- One slightly gross or uncomfortable detail (needle through the wound, pus under the skin). That is the hook.
- Last sentence is the resolution (healed, splinter out, payments cut off).

## What animation they use (inferred)
- Full 3D CGI: realistic human models with slightly stylised, smooth skin; rendered in a 3D package (Blender / Maya class), made by a team of animators.
- Two kinds of shot, alternating: (a) a person in a normal place doing the thing, (b) a cut-away cross-section of skin / body showing what happens inside.
- Slow camera pushes, soft studio lighting, clean backgrounds, a new shot roughly every 2-3 seconds.
- Small captions or none; the picture carries it. Light sound effects (squelch, snip), soft music.

## What the numbers say
- Body processes that everyone has felt (stitches, splinter) get 10-50x the views of the story or the concept video.
- The shortest, simplest scripts did best. So: one common body event, said to "you", shown inside the skin.

## Limits on our side (honest)
- Real animated 3D humans at this level cannot be rendered in this workspace.
- What can be done: realistic 3D-render-style still images for each shot (AI-generated), kept consistent, then animated: slow camera moves, cross-fades between stages, moving blood cells / particles drawn on top.
- Result: the same look and pacing shot by shot, but characters will not walk or move their limbs like a true 3D animation.
